# Source and confidence protocol

## Campsites and prices

Use official campsite/operator descriptions for facilities and policies, official tourism/park pages for corroboration, and a current official or authorized booking flow for prices and inventory. Third-party summaries may locate candidates but do not override operator terms. Open the actual source; search snippets alone are not proof. Record source URL, what it supports, and checked-at time with timezone.

For each option record dates, party/pitch/vehicle assumptions, surface and size, amenities, arrival window, accessibility and pets if relevant, drive estimate, cancellation terms, booking link, currency, taxes/fees and quote scope. Show unresolved requirements as unknown, not as satisfied. Distinct pitches at one campground do not count as distinct campsites unless the user requests pitch-level alternatives.

A dated rate must cover the actual selected unit and party. Separate:
- `available`: a dated inventory check showed a matching unit; not held or reserved;
- `unavailable`: matching inventory explicitly unavailable at that check;
- `unknown`: not checked, blocked, ambiguous, or not exposed;
- `request_required`: operator requires a request before confirmation.

Show the exact timestamp and scope of checks. Dynamic inventory may change immediately. Never claim a reservation from search results or an availability badge.

Calculate each night's rate separately when rates vary. Whole stay = nightly subtotal − eligible discount + known fees/taxes; list excluded/unknown fees separately. “Half price” means half the eligible amount only, not automatically half the whole booking. Verify conditions such as second-night-only, weekdays, membership, minimum stay, pitch type, occupancy and promotion validity. Show regular eligible amount, discount arithmetic, and final quoted total; unknown eligibility is not an applied discount. Compare like-for-like costs, distinguishing per-person, per-pitch and whole-group prices.

## Weather and route confidence

Prefer the official national/regional weather authority. Label the forecast place/station, elevation if known, issue/check time, relevant local dates and forecast horizon. A town forecast is not a mountain/campsite measurement; explicitly note elevation/exposure mismatch and seek a mountain forecast or official warnings where relevant. Beyond the forecast horizon, describe historical climate as climate, not a forecast. Missing precipitation probability is **unknown**, never 0%; rainfall amount and probability are different measures.

Map icons to the documented forecast conditions; retain the text and units so icons do not overstate precision. Include temperature, rain/wind and warnings when supported, and a practical user-agreed go/no-go fallback. Surface official closure or hazard advisories. No forecast guarantees safety.

Use a routing source for drive estimates and state origin-area, route mode, timestamp and traffic assumptions. Straight-line distance is not road distance. Do not invent exact times or distances to toilets/water from an unscaled campsite sketch; label them approximate only if a defensible basis exists, otherwise unknown.

## Capabilities and fallbacks

Use a documented connector, browser, search or API supported in the environment. Do not hardcode reverse-engineered provider endpoints as stable contracts. If a page blocks access or requires login, describe the gap and provide the booking link/manual-check instructions. Ask for a user-provided quote or screenshot when useful, labeling it as supplied evidence with its date. Never bypass an access restriction or treat a failed check as “sold out.”

When live tools are absent, deliver an interview/plan framework or source-check checklist, not purported current researched recommendations. If fewer than the requested number meet constraints, show the verified count and near-misses with explicit failing criteria; ask before expanding the route cap or changing accommodation type.
